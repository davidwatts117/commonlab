Running the Bench

The vessel already holds a measured portion of one solution, and the burette is topped up with the other. Squeeze the tap open to let drops fall and let go to pause. When the burette reaches the bottom the run stops on its own.

Getting Sensible Volumes

The equivalence volume scales with the ratio of the two concentrations. For a comfortable run, keep the solution in the burette roughly 1¼ to 5× as concentrated as the one in the vessel. The suggested ranges under each concentration box update to keep the jump on the chart.

volume at equivalence  ≈  25 mL × (concentration in vessel ÷ concentration in burette)

The Equivalence Point

The two solutions have reacted completely when the moles of acid equal the moles of base. The pH at that moment depends on what is left behind in solution.

strong + strong → neutral (pH 7)
weak acid + strong base → basic (pH > 7)
strong acid + weak base → acidic (pH < 7)

Choosing a Colour Indicator

Each indicator flips colour across a span of about two pH units. Pick one whose colour change lands on the vertical part of the curve, otherwise the colour change you see will not match the true equivalence point.

Crystal violet 0.0–1.8  •  Methyl orange 3.3–4.5
Erichrome black T 5.0–6.5  •  Bromothymol blue 6.0–7.5
Phenolphthalein 8.0–10.0  •  Alizarin yellow 10.0–12.0

About the Numbers

The pH is found by solving the full equilibrium relations — a quadratic away from the jump and a cubic across it — so dilute and very weak acids stay accurate instead of relying on the usual short-cut. Pairing a weak acid with a weak base is left out because it needs a quartic.
