How Titration Works

A solution of known concentration (the titrant) is added to a solution of unknown concentration (the analyte) until the reaction is complete. The equivalence point is reached when moles of acid = moles of base.

Equivalence Point

MaVa = MbVb

For a 1:1 reaction, moles of acid = moles of base at the equivalence point. The pH at this point depends on the acid/base strength.

pH at Equivalence

Strong-Strong: pH = 7 (neutral)
Weak Acid + Strong Base: pH > 7 (basic)
Strong Acid + Weak Base: pH < 7 (acidic)
The indicator should change colour near the equivalence point pH.

Choosing an Indicator

An indicator is only useful if its transition range sits inside the steep jump at the equivalence point. Switch indicators and watch which one actually changes colour at the right moment.

Reading the Flask

There is deliberately no pH meter by default. In a real lab you cannot see the pH — the only evidence is the colour of the indicator in the flask, and your titre is whatever reading you had when the colour changed.

Show pH turns on the meter, the titration curve and the pH scale if you want to watch the chemistry instead. Bear in mind that it hands you the endpoint you would otherwise have to find.
