Period of a Pendulum

T = 2π√(L/g)

For small angles (< 15°), the period depends only on length and gravity — not on mass or amplitude. This is isochronism.

Energy Dynamics

Etot = K + P + Ethermal

Energy transitions smoothly between Kinetic (K) and Potential (P). Air drag continuously dissipates mechanical energy into Thermal energy, so the Total line stays flat even as the pendulum slows.

Large-Angle Correction

T ≈ 2π√(L/g) · (1 + θ²/16)

For angles above ~15°, the small-angle approximation breaks down. The true period increases slightly with amplitude.

Explore

Try this: Set the drag coefficient to Cd = 0.00 for an ideal, non-damped pendulum and watch K and P swap forever with a flat Total line. Raise it to 0.47 (a real sphere in air) or 0.94 (twice normal air drag) to watch Thermal climb while Total stays flat — energy is conserved, just converted.
